Fever and rash in children

How to care for your child at home, protect others — and know when to get help

The 5 things to remember

  • Most rashes with fever are caused by viruses and get better on their own.
  • Do the glass test. If the spots do not fade under a glass, go to the emergency department now.
  • For fever, use paracetamol. Never give aspirin to children. Do not give ibuprofen for chickenpox.
  • Keep your child at home and away from pregnant women and newborn babies until your doctor says it is safe.
  • Check your child has had all their vaccines, including two doses of MMR.

The glass test

Press the side of a clear glass firmly on the spots. If you can still see the spots through the glass, this can be a sign of a serious infection called meningococcal disease (meningitis). Go to the emergency department or call 997 straight away. On darker skin, check the palms, soles and inside the eyelids.

Caring for your child at home

  • Give small drinks often — water, milk or breast milk. Cold drinks help a sore mouth.
  • Use paracetamol if your child is uncomfortable. Follow the dose on the pack for their age or weight.
  • Dress your child in light clothes. For itchy spots, use calamine lotion and keep nails short.
  • Wash hands often and use a separate towel. Finish all the antibiotic if your doctor gives one.

Protect others

  • Pregnant women who have been near your child should call their doctor today — some rashes can harm an unborn baby.
  • Keep your child away from newborn babies and anyone with weak immunity (for example, on cancer treatment or steroids).
  • Call the clinic before you come, so staff can keep other patients safe in the waiting room.

Get help now — call 997 or go to the emergency department if your child:

  • Has spots that do not fade with the glass test.
  • Is very sleepy, hard to wake, confused, or floppy.
  • Has a fit (seizure), a stiff neck, or fast or difficult breathing.
  • Has cold hands and feet with a high fever, or is not passing urine.
  • Is under 3 months old with a temperature of 38 °C or more.

See your doctor today if:

  • The fever lasts 5 days or more, especially with red eyes, red lips or swollen hands and feet.
  • Your child is not drinking enough, or a spot becomes very red, swollen or painful.
  • Your child has sickle cell disease or weak immunity and becomes pale or tired.
  • You live in or visited an area with dengue and your child has fever with body pain — do not give ibuprofen.
