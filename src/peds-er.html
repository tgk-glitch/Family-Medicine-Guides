Three changes that matter most at 3 am. PRBC is mL/kg, not mg/kg. Morphine is 0.1 mg/kg, not 1 mcg/kg. Granisetron IV is 0.01 mg/kg, not 0.1 mg/kg. All three are decimal or unit slips, the most common pediatric prescribing error.

Maintenance (Holliday–Segar 4-2-1)

Fluid: 0.9% NaCl or Plasma-Lyte + D5 + KCl 20 mEq/L (AAP 2018, age 28 days–18 years). Not for neonates, heart/kidney/liver failure, DI, burns or neurosurgery. Adult cap ~100 mL/h.

Bolus

Write rate and duration in the order comment. Reassess HR, CRT, BP, liver edge and lung crackles after every bolus. Without ICU access and no hypotension, avoid boluses in febrile illness (FEAST).

KCl check

Your formula: KCl × rate × 24 ÷ 500 ÷ weight. Here the 500 becomes whatever bag you pick. Add K only after urine output is confirmed (except DKA with K <3.5, per ISPAD).

Packed red cells

Formula assumes PRBC Hct ~60% (factor 4; use 5 for Hct ~70%). Sickle cell: do not raise Hb above 10 g/dL (hyperviscosity). Chronic severe anemia: give 5 mL/kg aliquots slowly with furosemide.

Albumin

25% albumin = 0.25 g/mL. Watch BP and breathing during the infusion; stop if crackles or hypertension.

Hypoglycemia

Treat glucose <70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) with symptoms, or <54 mg/dL. Recheck in 15 min. Draw critical sample (insulin, cortisol, GH, BHB, FFA) before treating if no known cause.

Infusion rate

Rate (mL/h) = dose × weight × 60 ÷ concentration. Enter your pharmacy's standard syringe concentration; the default is an example only.

Which vasoactive

  • Cold shock (cool peripheries, weak pulses, low output): epinephrine first.
  • Warm shock (bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure): norepinephrine first.
  • Catecholamine-resistant, low output with adequate BP: add milrinone.
  • Start through a peripheral line or IO if central access is delayed; do not wait. (SSC Children 2020)
  • Give hydrocortisone if fluid- and vasoactive-refractory, or adrenal risk (chronic steroids).

Convulsive status epilepticus

Start the clock at seizure onset. Glucose check at any step.

PECARN · minor head injury, GCS 14–15, <24 h

Severe mechanism: MVC with ejection, rollover or a death in the vehicle; pedestrian or unhelmeted cyclist hit by a vehicle; fall >0.9 m (<2 y) or >1.5 m (≥2 y); head struck by a high-impact object. Rates are clinically important TBI in the derivation cohort. (Kuppermann, Lancet 2009)

Board three-liner. 3-year-old, 14 kg, wheeze and PRAM 7 after one salbutamol neb. Give salbutamol 2.5 mg + ipratropium 250 mcg every 20 min × 3, prednisolone 28 mg once daily for 3 days, reassess PRAM at 60 min; if still ≥8, magnesium 700 mg IV over 20 min and call PICU.
